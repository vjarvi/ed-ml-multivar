Emergency department crowding worsens clinical outcomes and strains staff and resources. This thesis examined whether recently released foundational time series models can forecast hourly ED occupancy accurately enough in a zero-shot setting to be useful in practice. Using Tampere University Hospital’s ED "Acuta" data and the experimental protocol aligned with prior work, five open-source FMs were compared against established baselines and assessed how context length, external covariates, and computational requirements affect their use.

First, we evaluated predictive accuracy. All univariate FMs except Chronos moderately outperformed the strongest baseline (LightGBM). TimesFM achieved the best overall accuracy, while TiRex, Sundial, and Moirai were close when given similarly long histories. Chronos underperformed, likely it being the oldest model~\cite{amazon_chronos_bolt_base}. Increasing context length consistently improved accuracy across models, with diminishing returns beyond roughly 2,048 hours. These results show that zero-shot FMs can deliver competitive accuracy for ED occupancy forecasting.

Forecasting with external covariates was tested. From the models used, only Moirai had the ability to include covariates in its prediction. Results were significantly worse than the simple seasonal naïve baseline. The model failed to use covariates, and no general conclusions can be made on the use of external covariates in ED occupancy forecasting.

Finally, we measured computational costs. Moirai was the most memory-efficient model. Chronos produced the fastest inferences at short contexts, while TiRex and Moirai were fastest at longer contexts. Inference times for all models were practical for hourly operational use on modern hardware. These findings indicate that zero-shot FMs can reduce the operational burden associated with frequent model retraining, enabling faster experimentation and easier deployment across sites.

This work has limitations. We studied a single hospital over a fixed period and evaluated one FM with multivariate inputs. Model finetuning and careful selection of covariates were out of scope. Future research should (1) replicate across multiple hospitals and time periods; (2) test effect of fine-tuning; and (3) investigate covariate selection possibly with future models that support external covariates.

In conclusion, foundational time series models already provide accurate, fast, and operationally attractive zero-shot forecasts for ED occupancy. With long historical context, several models cluster in performance and surpass strong baselines, substantially lowering the barrier to adoption by removing per-site training. While multivariate zero-shot use did not help in our setting, careful covariate design and light adaptation may close that gap. Taken together, these results support zero-shot FMs as a practical starting point for ED forecasting and outline a path toward even stronger, fine-tuned and reliable deployments.
